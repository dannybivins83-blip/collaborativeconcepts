## 1. TL;DR

Two companies are exploring a "partnership vendor" arrangement around **permanent
building-access and fall-protection systems** (davit arms, anchors, tracks, power
platforms) and the compliance work that surrounds them:

- **BA** *(a.k.a. "B access")* — the **manufacturer**. Designs, builds, and
  load-tests the equipment. Explicitly manufacturer-only: it does **not** stamp
  field installs, pull-test in the field, or certify buildings.
- **La Gala Construction / UBS** — the CC-side team. Brings the **opportunities,
  field install, third-party stamped engineering, pull tests, and the annual WWS
  inspections**. UBS is a multi-division inspection/testing firm with brick-and-mortar
  offices in every major US city.

The proposed split: **La Gala/UBS carries the install + structural + certification
liability; BA stays manufacturer-of-record for the hardware only.** The
go-to-market wedge is the largely-unenforced **OSHA Walking-Working Surfaces
standard**, driven through HOA/condo compliance and milestone (Ch. 558)
recertification work.

## 3. The deal being explored ("partnership vendor")

**Core idea:** La Gala/UBS is in the field and knows the owners, GCs, and buildings;
BA has the engineered product. Pair them so BA gets opportunities and La Gala/UBS
does everything from the base of the anchor down (structure, install, cert,
inspection).

- **Opportunity flow:** La Gala/UBS (and sales guys like **Greg** *(verify)*) bring
  BA into bids / buildings they're already on. If La Gala/UBS surfaces a deal they
  don't want to execute, they still take a **finder's / referral fee**.
- **Liability split (the key point):**
  - **BA's liability ends at the base of the davit / pedestal** — the hardware.
  - **La Gala/UBS owns the structure, the install, and the certification** —
    backed by **third-party PE stamps**. This is framed as "sharing burden" and
    "taking the liability off" BA.
  - **BA can be engineer-of-record for the equipment design**; UBS provides the
    structural stamp + physical pull-test + sign-off. Alternatively, UBS **trains
    La Gala crews** to perform inspections and UBS stamps them.
- **Annual WWS inspections** become recurring revenue: someone must inspect,
  service, and re-certify permanently-installed anchors/davits/tracks every year.
- **Temporary-side tension (unresolved):** La Gala/UBS floated buying equipment
  (swing stages, motors, davits) to build a **shared rental fleet** and "open a
  rental division." BA pushed back hard — *"so we can be like Sky Climber and
  compete against our own customers."* BA will sell **permanent** systems to
  qualified PE dealers (La Gala could be one) but is protective of the temporary
  dealer channel. **This is the main open commercial disagreement.**
- **Contracting mechanics:** whoever is the **prime** on a given job dictates the
  paperwork; the B ↔ La Gala relationship is set per-contract. Expect to sometimes
  sub under a prime (e.g., **ProMax** *(verify)*) and to keep the "La Gala" name off
  certain jobs where the prime is sensitive. NDA to be put in place; bonds and
  warranties handled per job (owner approves the warranty; higher premiums attach).

## 4. Go-to-market wedge — the OSHA Walking-Working Surfaces (WWS) standard

This is the engine the whole partnership is meant to run on, and it maps directly
to the existing `/wwslgc/` content.

- **What it is:** OSHA's ~2016/2017 Walking-Working Surfaces update (Subpart D/I,
  adapted in part from Cal-OSHA and NYC) requires **permanently installed** access
  and fall-protection assets — davit arms, anchors, monorail/track systems,
  tiebacks, guardrails, fixed ladders, catwalks, roof-access points — to be
  **inspected, serviced, and staff-trained on a yearly basis** (a ~40-page
  standard). Painted-over anchors/ladders/bolts must be re-tested.
- **Enforcement reality:**
  - **Largely unenforced in Florida** — the state reportedly pulled OSHA personnel
    off WWS onto ground-level work and blocked enforcement. This is the *gap* the
    business sells into (compliance liability exists even where enforcement lags).
  - **Heavily enforced** in NY, Chicago, Atlanta, Houston, LA, and **Canada**
    (CSA — e.g. bird-droppings on aluminum = acidic corrosion = ticketable).
  - **California** is the strictest (its own Cal-OSHA + ASME A120 checklists; can
    fail a building for one out-of-date item; retrofits triggered on inspection).
- **Demand drivers:** Surfside collapse raised attention; **Florida milestone /
  40-year recertification** (SB-4D era) overlaps with WWS; HOA/condo boards and
  "unsafe building boards" (e.g., Hollywood) are targets.
- **Channel partner:** an attorney — **Robbie** *(possibly "Robbie Blanche")* at
  **Siegfried Rivera** *(verify)*, described as a large HOA/condo law firm managing
  ~**700 buildings** — writes HOA compliance contracts and is "waiting for the
  green light" to send compliance letters that generate WWS work.

## 6. Product / technical notes (from the PE walkthrough)

Useful context for scoping jobs and for `/wwslgc/` content accuracy:

- **Power platforms / stages:** CCB level control, upper & lower limit sensors,
  secondary wire-rope, overload sensor, tilt sensor, anemometer, cable basket,
  mesh screening (a **California requirement**). BA can **retrofit** an overload
  sensor onto existing stages.
- **Davit arms / "gabbetts":** **bottom-rotating** (cheaper, ground-rig) vs
  **top-rotating** (roof-launch). Sized to the building. Rough figures *(verify)*:
  6×6 ~**$4–5k**; a larger ~11-ft unit ~**$6,500**.
- **Anchors:** post-install (Hilti epoxy — e.g., **HIT-RE 500** *(verify)* —
  expansion, or self-tapping concrete anchors), **wall anchors** (tie back to
  structural walls), **embed** styles (tie to rebar and pour concrete around),
  **flush-mount pedestals** (hidden under pavers), **cover plates** (stainless,
  ~$250) for water protection, and **track-mounted** davits.
- **Load testing:** each loop tested to **5,000 lb** (double-tie plate can see
  ~10,000 lb; ~2,500 lb per point), stated **2:1** factor. **BA tests to ultimate
  in-house; a PE must field-test and sign off** — again the liability handoff.
- **Roof-launch thresholds *(verify)*:** roof-launch generally required above
  **~490–500 ft** nationally; **California** roof-launches above ~300 ft and
  requires a power platform on buildings over ~100 ft; **BMU** territory above
  ~500 ft. Developers game **start dates** to beat code (example cited: a ~757-unit
  / ~850-ft tower).
- **International:** for the Caribbean (Bermuda/Bahamas), **stainless everything**
  (washers, drop-in anchors, epoxy) to survive salt corrosion; annual recert flights
  ~$5k/yr.
