Madeira Beach Development

A non-conforming double-lot assembled and entitled for residential development.

Active — Raising Capital
Project Detail All Selected Work →

The situation

Coastal infill parcels rarely arrive clean. This one arrived as a question about what the land was legally permitted to become — not as a design brief.

In built-out beach communities, much of the land was platted under rules that no longer exist. Two adjoining lots can read as one obvious development site on a map while remaining two separate legal descriptions, each carrying its own frontage, setback, and use conditions inherited from an earlier code. None of that is unusual. What matters is whether the distance between what the plat says and what current regulation contemplates can be closed, and what closing it would actually require.

So the first work is not architectural. It is establishing, in writing and in the right order, what the parcel can support and what the approval path really looks like — before a single dollar is committed to a drawing that the code may not permit.

Questions we answer before anything else

  • What is of record — legal descriptions, plat history, easements, and any prior approvals attached to the parcel.
  • Which regulation governs today, and precisely where the parcel is non-conforming under it.
  • Whether the lots are better held separately or assembled, and what each path costs in time.
  • Which approvals are administrative, which are discretionary, and who actually decides them.
  • What would have to be true, in writing, for a lender or capital partner to underwrite the plan.

The approach

Prove the plan before spending money on it. Every step below exists to retire a specific risk, in the order that risk actually shows up.

Diligence before design

Drawings are cheap to produce and expensive to defend. The entitlement path is established first, and the design follows what that path will actually support — not the other way around.

One accountable line

Survey, land use, design, and capital are coordinated by the same party. Nothing gets handed across a gap on the assumption that somebody else already caught it.

Written, not remembered

Findings, conditions, and approvals are documented as they happen, so the record supporting the plan is assembled continuously rather than reconstructed under deadline.

What the work involves

The workstreams below run in sequence, with each one gating the next. This is the method applied to Madeira Beach and to development opportunities like it.

Record and title review

Assemble the plat history, legal descriptions, easements, and prior approvals into one reviewed record, then identify where those documents disagree with each other.

Code and entitlement mapping

Map the parcel against the land use and zoning regulation in force, isolate each non-conforming condition, and define the specific approval route that condition requires.

Consultant coordination

Engage licensed surveyors, engineers, and design professionals, scope their work to the questions that actually decide the project, and hold the schedule between them.

Assemblage and structure

Determine whether the lots are best held, combined, or reconfigured, and structure ownership so that the development plan and the capital plan describe the same thing.

Capital preparation

Assemble the diligence package, define the use of funds, and present the opportunity in the form a partner or lender can evaluate on its merits rather than on a narrative.

Execution management

Sequence approvals, permitting, and construction so the plan that was approved is the plan that gets built, with licensed trades performing the work and deviations surfaced early enough to address.
