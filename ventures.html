Venture and Sub-Site Policy

The role decides the treatment.

Ventures carry real value, and none of it survives if a visitor cannot tell a venture from a service line. So every related property is assigned one corporate role first, and the role determines how much of it belongs on this site.

A corporate role answers one question: what is this property to Collaborative Concept? The site treatment answers the next one: how much of it belongs on the corporate site, and in what form. Settling the first question makes the second mechanical rather than a matter of taste.

Nothing on the list below is a service you buy from Collaborative Concept. A venture runs under its own brand, on its own site, for its own audience. What appears here is a description of the relationship and, where the policy allows it, one case study — never a second set of service pages competing with the two divisions.

That is also why the link to this page sits in the footer rather than in the primary navigation. Placing ventures beside Development and Solutions would suggest they are a third thing the firm sells. They are not, and the navigation should not have to be explained.

The treatments are deliberately narrow: an independent site stays independent, a case study stays a single case study, and a selected-work entry stays one entry. Each is a ceiling, not a starting point.

Method

How a property gets classified.

Four steps, in the same order every time. The site only changes at the last one.

Name the Property

Identify the site or business exactly as it is branded, without folding it into a service line.

Assign the Role

Give it one corporate role: venture, venture product, partner proof, or Solutions deployment.

Set the Treatment

The role decides the ceiling: independent site, one case study, one selected-work entry, or a footer link.

State the Boundary

Write the relationship and any licensing, transaction, or referral limit into the entry itself.

A role can change. When it does, the status label and the treatment change with it — the surrounding copy is not rewritten to suggest more than the label says.

Boundary

Where ventures end and client work begins.

Ventures are operated separately from client engagements. That separation is the reason this page can exist at all.

A client engagement is scoped, priced, staffed, and measured on its own terms. A venture does not ride along inside it, is never a condition of working with the firm, and is not billed through it. Where a venture and an engagement could touch the same matter, the overlap is disclosed in writing before work begins and the client decides how to proceed.

Naming a property on this page describes a relationship, not a title. Ownership, partner interests, and transaction roles sit with the parties to them and are identified inside the entry rather than assumed from the fact that it is listed.

The same limits that govern the rest of the site govern this one. Collaborative Concept leads strategy, coordination, and oversight; it does not perform licensed work, and a venture does not create an exception to that.

Venture disclosure. Ventures are operated separately from Collaborative Concept client engagements. A venture is not a client service, is not staffed or billed through an engagement, and is never a condition of working with the firm.

Naming a venture or partner property on this page is not a claim of sole ownership, control, or a licensed role in it. Licensing, transaction, and referral boundaries are stated in each entry, and licensed work is performed by appropriately licensed professionals engaged for that scope.

What no entry states

No revenue, savings, recovery, or performance figure. No acquisition, wind-down, exit, or transaction. No claim of sole ownership, and no licensed role the firm does not hold. Where a number or an outcome would belong, it stays off the page until it has been verified and approved for publication.
