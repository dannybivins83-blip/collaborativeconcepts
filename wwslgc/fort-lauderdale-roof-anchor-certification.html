water_drop Salt air is the silent failure mode

A roof anchor a mile from the Atlantic lives a harder life than one inland. Chloride-laden coastal air drives corrosion of anchor bolts, base plates, and embedments long before it shows on the surface — the connection can look fine and still fail a pull test. That is exactly why a visual once-over is not enough on a Fort Lauderdale rooftop. Our field crews proof-load each anchor, inspect the substrate, and document the result, so corrosion is caught before it becomes a fall.

cleaning_services Window-washing & façade-access anchors

Any Broward building where workers suspend from the roof to clean glass or service the façade relies on certified anchorage. Rope descent system (RDS) anchors must be certified to support the loads set in OSHA 1910.27(b): a qualified-person inspection at least annually, and certification at intervals no greater than 10 years. Personal fall-arrest tie-offs fall under 1910.140 — inspected before each use (an annual documented inspection follows the ANSI Z359 recommendation, which is best practice, not a federal mandate). We handle roof anchor load testing in Fort Lauderdale and window-washing anchor certification across Broward for davit bases, tieback anchors, and continuous lifeline systems alike.

event_available Broward's recertification clock

Broward sets its own building-safety timeline. Under the Broward County Board of Rules and Appeals (BORA Policy 05-05), most buildings face a structural and electrical recertification at 25 years of age, then every 10 years. On top of that, Florida's statewide Milestone Inspection (FS 553.899) covers buildings of three or more habitable stories at 30 years — or 25 years within three miles of the coast by local determination — and every 10 years after. Certified, clearly documented rooftop anchorage keeps your maintenance and façade crews compliant straight through those cycles. Map the dates on our compliance calendar.

gavel What a missed anchor costs

OSHA penalties for 2026 run up to $16,550 per serious violation and up to $165,514 for a willful or repeat citation — per item, before any injury liability or insurance fallout. For a Fort Lauderdale property manager, an uncertified or failing roof anchor is both a life-safety exposure and a six-figure financial one. Getting the anchorage tested, certified, and — where needed — repaired is the cheap side of that ledger.
