# Public-Records Request Playbook — South Florida Permit & Lead Data

Everything the online feeds can't give you, obtained the legal way. Florida's
Public Records Act (**Chapter 119, F.S.**) requires every county and city to
hand over existing records — including database exports — at no more than the
actual cost of duplication. Cities cannot ask *why* you want them.

**How to use this file**
1. Pick the record type (permits, violations, notices of commencement, etc.).
2. Copy the template, fill in the **[JURISDICTION]** blanks.
3. Send to that jurisdiction's public-records contact (table at the bottom) —
   or use its "Public Records Request" web form, which always reaches the right
   custodian. When an exact email is unknown, the web form is the safe route.
4. Ask for **CSV/Excel** and a **recurring monthly export** — the tool already
   ingests CSV/Excel (that's how Boca Raton works), so returned files wire
   straight in.

## ⚠️ Before you use owner phone/email — read this

These requests ask for **owner/applicant/contractor phone and email** (permit
apps capture them, and they're generally public record). But **obtaining a number
is not consent to call or text it.**

- **Direct mail** to the site/mailing address → **safe**, needs no consent. This
  is the primary channel — it's what the WWSLGC collateral (flyer, postcard,
  Subpart D checklist) is built for.
- **Manual, individually-dialed calls** to a **contractor/business** line →
  lower risk.
- **Autodialed/prerecorded calls, cold texts, or bulk email to homeowners** →
  **high liability.** Florida's **FTSA** (§501.059) and the federal **TCPA**
  require **prior express written consent** and carry **$500–$1,500 per
  message**; Florida sees heavy FTSA class-action litigation. A phone number
  from a public record gives **zero** consent.
- Scrub any call/text list against the **National DNC Registry** and honor
  STOP/opt-outs (the WWSLGC signature already includes an opt-out line).

Bottom line: request the contact fields (great for verification, skip-tracing,
and mail), but **run outreach as direct mail** unless you have written consent.
This is guidance, not legal advice — confirm your call/text program with counsel.

## A) Building-permit export (the core request — send to every jurisdiction)

> **Subject:** Public Records Request — Building Permit Data Export
>
> Under Chapter 119, Florida Statutes, I request an electronic export of building
> permits issued by **[JURISDICTION]** from **January 1, 2024 to present**, with:
> permit/record number; permit type and description (scope of work); site address,
> city, ZIP; parcel/folio (PCN); application date; issue date; status; declared
> job valuation; contractor name, license #, phone, and email; applicant name,
> phone, and email; and property-owner name, mailing address, phone, and email —
> **as maintained in the permit record**, with any information exempt under
> §119.071, F.S. redacted as you deem required.
>
> Please provide as **CSV or Excel (.xlsx)** — a database export, not PDFs. If a
> **recurring monthly export** of newly issued permits is possible, I'd like to
> set that up. Notify me of any duplication cost over $25 before processing.

*(The "phone and email … as maintained, with §119.071 exemptions redacted"
wording is deliberate: it asks for the contact fields, and pre-authorizes the
agency to redact the legally-exempt ones so they can't use "we'd have to redact"
as a reason to deny the whole request. Contractor contact info is almost always
released; owner/applicant phone/email is a mix by agency.)*

Highest-value targets not already in the tool: **City of Miami**, **Miami Beach**,
**Hollywood**, **Pompano Beach**, **Coral Springs**, **Davie**, **Boynton Beach**,
plus **unincorporated Palm Beach / Broward / Miami-Dade counties**.

## B) Code-enforcement / open-violation list  ⭐ best WWS leads

Open code cases = distressed properties that legally *must* fix something — often
unsafe structures, roofs, and railings. (This is exactly the Miami-Dade EnerGov
violations feed, but every city keeps its own.)

> **Subject:** Public Records Request — Open Code Enforcement Cases
>
> Under Ch. 119, F.S., I request an export of **open/active code-enforcement
> cases** for **[JURISDICTION]** as of today, with: case number; site address,
> city, ZIP; parcel/folio; violation type/description; status; date opened; last
> activity date; and any lien reference. **CSV or Excel** preferred; recurring
> monthly export if available.

Ask specifically for **unsafe-structure, structural, roof, guardrail/railing, and
building-code** violation categories if they can filter — those are the anchor-
recert and structural-repair leads.

---

## C) Expired / open (un-finaled) permits  ⭐ recert + close-out leads

Properties with permits that were never closed out — owners need them finaled,
and roof/structural ones map directly to recertification work.

> **Subject:** Public Records Request — Expired and Open (Un-Finaled) Permits
>
> Under Ch. 119, F.S., I request an export of permits in **[JURISDICTION]** that
> are currently **expired or open/not finaled**, with: permit number; type and
> description; site address, city, ZIP; parcel/folio; issue date; expiration date;
> status; contractor; and owner name/mailing address if maintained. **CSV/Excel.**

---

## D) Notices of Commencement (from the County Clerk/Recorder) ⭐ earliest signal

An NOC is recorded **before** permitted work over $2,500 starts — it names the
owner AND the contractor, weeks ahead of the permit showing in any feed. Recorded
at the **county Clerk/Recorder**, not the city.

> **Subject:** Public Records Request — Notices of Commencement
>
> Under Ch. 119, F.S., I request an export of **Notices of Commencement** recorded
> in **[COUNTY]** from **January 1, 2025 to present**, with: recording date;
> document/instrument number; property legal description and/or parcel; property
> address; owner name; and contractor/preparer name. **CSV or Excel** preferred;
> recurring monthly export if available.

Send to: **Miami-Dade Clerk of Courts** (Recording), **Broward County Records,
Taxes & Treasury** (Recording Division), **Palm Beach County Clerk & Comptroller**
(Official Records), **Martin County Clerk of Court** (Recording). Several of these
already publish Official Records search online — the records request gets you a
bulk export instead of one-at-a-time lookups.

---

## E) Unsafe-structure / condemnation / demolition list ⭐ structural leads

> **Subject:** Public Records Request — Unsafe Structure & Condemnation Records
>
> Under Ch. 119, F.S., I request records of properties in **[JURISDICTION]**
> declared **unsafe, unsound, or condemned**, or with an **open demolition or
> unsafe-structure order**, in the last 24 months, with: case/record number; site
> address, city, ZIP; parcel/folio; order type; status; and date. **CSV/Excel.**

For Miami-Dade, also ask for the **40-year / 50-year recertification** and
**Building Recertification non-compliance** lists — those are the highest-intent
recert leads in the county.

## F) (Optional) New business tax receipts — commercial leads

> **Subject:** Public Records Request — Business Tax Receipts Issued
>
> Under Ch. 119, F.S., I request an export of **Business Tax Receipts issued** by
> **[JURISDICTION]** in the last 12 months, with: business name; business address;
> classification/type; issue date; and owner/applicant name. **CSV/Excel.**
