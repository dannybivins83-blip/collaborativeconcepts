Driveway gates, walk gates, and gate repair

Gates are the part of a fence that moves, so they're the part that fails. A gate that was built square, hung on the right hardware, and set on posts sized for the load will open the same way in year ten as it did on day one.

What we build

  • Walk gates in wood, vinyl, aluminum, and chain link, matched to the fence they sit in.
  • Double drive gates for driveways, side yards, and equipment access.
  • Rolling and cantilever slide gates where there isn't room for a gate to swing.
  • Pool gates with self-closing hinges and self-latching hardware positioned to meet the code.
  • Gates sized for an operator, if you're adding or already have an automatic opener.

Why gates sag — and how we prevent it

A gate leaf is a lever. All of its weight hangs off the hinge side, and the longer and heavier the leaf, the more force it puts on that post every time it swings. Three things prevent sag:

  • An oversized hinge post, set deeper, in a bigger footing. A gate post is not a line post. Using the same post for both is the number one cause of gates that drag by the second season.
  • A rigid frame. Diagonal bracing on wood gates, welded or bolted internal frames inside vinyl and aluminum leaves. A rectangle without a diagonal is a hinge, not a frame.
  • Hardware rated for the actual weight, in a finish that survives coastal air. Undersized hinges will pull out and stainless or hot-dip galvanized is worth what it costs here.

Gate repair

We repair gates on fences we didn't build. Common calls: a gate that dragged a groove into the driveway, a hinge post leaning toward the opening, a latch that stopped catching, a pool gate that no longer self-closes, or a drive gate that racked out of square. Most are fixable in one visit — re-hanging, re-squaring, resetting the post, or replacing hardware. When a post is rotted or the footing has failed, we'll tell you that up front instead of hanging new hardware on something that won't hold it. More on fence repair →

Automatic gates

If you're planning an operator, the gate has to be built for it from the start — heavier frame, heavier posts, and clearances the operator needs. Tell us at the estimate and we'll build it to accept one, whether that's now or later.

Gates FAQs

Common questions

My gate drags on the ground. Can it be fixed?

Usually, yes. It's most often the hinge post leaning or the leaf racking out of square, and both can be corrected. If the post itself is rotted or the footing has broken up, the post needs replacing — we'll tell you which one you're looking at before we start.

How wide can a driveway gate be?

Wide, if the posts and hardware are sized for it. Past a certain width a double gate or a slide gate is the better answer than one enormous leaf, both for the hardware and for how it feels to open.

Do pool gates have different requirements?

Yes. A gate that's part of a pool barrier has to swing outward away from the pool, close by itself, latch by itself, and have the release positioned out of a small child's reach. Standard gate hardware doesn't meet that.
