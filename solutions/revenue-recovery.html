Revenue Recovery Sprint

You already paid for the leads. We help you make them pay you back.

A staged engagement built around one idea: the work you already bought is still sitting in your systems, and the way to reach it is a process, not a push.

The engagement

Four stages, run in sequence.

Most contractors do not have a lead problem. They have a follow-up problem wearing a lead problem's clothes. The sprint works the history you already own — in the order that keeps the work honest: understand it, rank it, act on it, then make it visible.

Stage One

Diagnose

Read the history already sitting in your systems.

Stage Two

Prioritize

Rank what is still open, and say why.

Stage Three

Relaunch

Rebuild outreach, cadence, and ownership.

Stage Four

Measure

Make the pipeline visible and write the process down.

Three questions we ask before anything changes.

They sound simple. In most shops the answers are different depending on who you ask, and that difference is the whole problem.

Who owns follow-up?

Not who is willing to do it — who is accountable for it by name, on a specific day, for a specific list. If the answer is "everybody", the answer is nobody.

How fast do you respond?

Not the number you would like to say out loud — the interval your own records show between an inbound lead and the first real attempt to reach a human.

What happens to unsold estimates?

An estimate that did not close is not a dead record. It is a priced conversation with a buyer who once raised a hand. Most of them are never touched again.

What actually happens

Inside the sequence.

Stage One

Diagnose

We pull the lead, estimate, and job history out of the CRM, the spreadsheets, and the inbox, and read it as one dataset instead of three. Where records enter, where they stall, where follow-up stops, and which stages nobody has been maintaining.

  • Reconcile records across the systems you already run
  • Map the path a lead takes today, including the informal steps
  • Identify where records go quiet and what they have in common

Stage Two

Prioritize

Every open record gets scored the same way — recency, scope, how far it got, and whether anyone ever reached a decision-maker. Then it is sorted into call lists your team can work from the top down without deciding who to call next.

  • A written scoring method, so the ranking can be argued with and repeated
  • Lists split by what the conversation should actually be about
  • Records retired on purpose instead of quietly abandoned

Stage Three

Relaunch

We write the outreach — call openers, voicemail, email and text — in language that fits your trade and your market, then set the cadence: who contacts whom, on which day, through which channel, and what happens on no answer. Your team runs it while we sit in.

  • Messaging drafted for the list, not generic templates
  • A cadence with named owners and defined stop conditions
  • Live working sessions while the first calls go out

Stage Four

Measure

We stand up a pipeline view that shows stage, owner, and aging in one place, review the results with your team, and hand over a findings report: what we found, what we changed, what should keep running, and what we would look at next.

  • A dashboard your team can read without a translator
  • A written process document, so the sprint survives the sprint
  • A closing review with the owner and whoever runs follow-up

What you get.

  • Database analysisOne reconciled view of leads, estimates, and jobs, assembled from the systems you already pay for.
  • Ranked call listsOpen records sorted by a scoring method we write down and hand over, so the ranking outlives us.
  • MessagingCall openers, voicemail, email and text drafts written for your trade, your market, and the specific list they belong to.
  • Follow-up cadenceWho contacts whom, on what day, through which channel, and the point at which a record is retired on purpose.
  • Pipeline dashboardA live view of stages, owners, and aging, so the next stall is visible while it is still cheap.

Who this is built for

  • You spend real money on lead generation and cannot say what happened to most of it.
  • Estimates go out, and whether they get followed up depends on who happens to remember.
  • Your CRM holds records nobody has opened since the day they were entered.
  • Your team is busy, so anything new has to be simple enough to actually get used.

What this engagement is — and is not.

The Revenue Recovery Sprint delivers a defined process and a defined set of deliverables: the database analysis, the ranked call lists, the messaging, the follow-up cadence, the pipeline dashboard, and the findings report described on this page.

It does not include, imply, or promise a guaranteed revenue outcome. What happens after the work is delivered depends on your market, your pricing, your capacity, and how consistently your team runs the process once it is handed over. We publish no recovery percentages and make no earnings claims.

Collaborative Concept is not acting as your broker, attorney, accountant, or contractor in this engagement.
